# August 10: optimal stirling engine crankshaft linkage

Motiongen is a great tool to experiment linkages. I learned that stirling engines should have a phase difference of 90 degrees. In other words, the displacer and power piston positions should be offset by 90 degrees, with the displacer being ahead. Using linkage simulations like Motiongen made this clearer for me! At first, I made a couple of small mistakes, such as putting the power piston ahead, switching their positions, etc. I found the importance of prototyping and experimenting.

# Sept 16th:
Building stirling engine (lapse)


Today I assembled the printed parts together to see how everything fit!
While the rhombic drive moved alright, there were some flaws that could be fixed. For example, there was too much friction, I learned, because the axles of the gears were directly rubbing against the edge of the 3d-printed gear holder. Additionally, when the linkages from the rhombic drive were closest together, they would sag a little. I found that the rhombic drive relied on tight geometry----my CAD was not restrictive enough, allowing for play in the linkage.

# Sept 17th: BETA1

During my research, I found that bearings were a great way to reduce friction in rotation! It's interesting how many different types of 3d printed print-in-place bearings there are. I decided to use the dumbbell configuration (instead of ball or roller), because it was easier to print and reduced friction. It was a game of testing tolerances, sizes, and revolving in Onshape. Lots of revolving.
